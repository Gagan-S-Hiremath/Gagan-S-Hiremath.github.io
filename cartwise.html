PHASE 1 OF 5

Discover

STEP 01Independent design · proposed validation

Problem Definition

A shopper choosing a commuter backpack must compare laptop compatibility, budget, weather protection, and availability across multiple pages. Filters narrow the catalog, but the shopper still has to interpret specifications and resolve trade-offs.

Product objective: Help shoppers form a suitable shortlist with less effort than browsing alone.

STEP 02User assumptions · discovery proposed

User Empathy and Discovery

The shopper needs a suitable product and a reason to trust the recommendation. A low price is not useful if the laptop does not fit. Merchants need accurate product claims, current inventory, and a smooth checkout handoff.

  • Observe shoppers selecting a backpack using conventional filters.
  • Identify abandoned searches, repeated comparisons, and non-negotiable constraints.
  • Test whether conversation adds value versus a better filter interface before committing to an agent.

No completed customer interviews, survey results or testimonials are claimed.

STEP 03Product decision rationale

Why AI Was Appropriate

Natural-language understanding converts the shopper’s request into explicit constraints. Retrieval finds matching products. The model explains catalog-backed trade-offs and asks for missing preferences. Deterministic filters enforce budget, compatibility, price, and inventory.

Alternative considered

Keep conventional filters and a comparison table. Evaluate whether conversation improves complex decisions enough to justify additional interaction.

Bound the model’s role; use deterministic checks for eligibility, explicit constraints and state integrity. Human accountability remains part of the product.

STEP 07Product requirements · artifact available

AI PRD

Users: English-speaking shoppers selecting a commuter backpack.

Requirements: Use catalog-backed specifications; preserve hard constraints; expose trade-offs; handle unavailable stock; recheck price and availability at handoff.

Failure handling: No valid match → explain and ask to revise constraints. Missing laptop dimensions → ask, not guess. Price/stock change → disclose before proceeding.

Non-functional requirements — proposed: access boundaries, auditable versions, bounded retries, task-specific latency/cost budgets and accountable support.

Dependencies: a structured, current catalog, complete constraint fields, merchant handoff contracts and representative user tasks.

Design & Plan

STEP 09Proposed architecture

Solution Architecture

Proposed MVP: one synthetic backpack catalog; required structured fields; retrieval plus constraint filters; evidence-linked comparison; cart preview. Checkout is a handoff design, not an autonomous payment capability.

Architecture choices and constraints.
Trade-offProduct decision
Personalization vs. frictionAsk one consequential question at a time. Do not require a long profile before showing useful options.
Fluent answers vs. factual claimsEvery recommendation references catalog attributes; missing features stay unknown.
Conversion vs. fitDo not sacrifice explicit constraints for a more expensive or higher-margin recommendation.

The language model interprets intent and explains options; deterministic constraints control eligibility. LangSmith could support future tracing and evaluation but is not claimed as an integration. Tracing explains execution; evaluation judges it against a standard.

STEP 12Proposed evaluation · no scored model runs

Offline Evaluation

Dataset

50 development shopping scenarios plus separate held-out scenarios; include no-match, missing specs, conflicting preferences, and adversarial requests.

Gold criteria

Label explicit hard constraints and the qualifying catalog set. Use human judgments for preference ranking.

Checks

Constraint satisfaction, grounded claims, price/stock correctness, ranking relevance, and cart-state correctness.

Regression

Evaluate catalog updates and prompt changes by task difficulty; re-run after inventory and policy changes.

STEP 13Proposed release plan

Go-Live Criteria

Proposed release: moderated prototype sessions → sandbox task study → one-category merchant pilot → gradual catalog expansion. Use the same catalog and product availability across experiment arms.

Proposed gate: no explicit hard-constraint violations or unsupported specifications in qualification; users can understand and correct preferences. Pilot expansion depends on observed task success, not a claimed conversion target.

Assign product and engineering owners; test access controls, review capacity, dependency failures and support before a pilot. Critical failures block release.

Rollback: Fall back to conventional filters on catalog outages, unsupported claims, or invalid cart states. Disable recommendation generation while preserving the product list and user-selected constraints.

PHASE 5 OF 5

Monitor & Improve

STEP 14Proposed monitoring · not production results

Online Evaluation

Task study

Randomize shoppers between conversational assistance and filters; counterbalance task order and use consistent catalogs.

User value

Measure successful selection, time to shortlist, perceived effort, and post-task confidence.

Behavior

Track accepted recommendations, changes to constraints, handoff and abandonment; distinguish curiosity from purchase intent.

Guardrails

Audit wrong-fit complaints and unsupported product claims. Real sales uplift requires merchant traffic and a powered experiment.

Use privacy-conscious traces with access controls, redaction and retention limits. Monitor task quality, latency, cost and changed input patterns separately. LangSmith is an optional proposed improvement.

STEP 15Unmeasured outcomes · pilot plan

Business Outcome Monitoring

This study defines a testable shopping workflow and a filter-based comparison experience. Customer interviews, model evaluations, and purchase experiments remain planned.

In a future merchant pilot, connect suitable shortlist completion and comparison effort with handoff intent and wrong-fit feedback. No purchase or conversion uplift is claimed.

Compare equivalent cohorts, record the baseline and measurement window, and predeclare experimental allocation and sample-size needs before making causal claims.

STEP 16Feedback-loop design

Learning and Iteration

Conversation is valuable only when it improves the selection. The next decision is whether clarification reduces wrong-fit choices enough to justify additional turns.

Production signal → failure analysis → root cause → requirement or test → backlog item → offline regression → release decision → monitoring.

Illustrative priority: a recommendation that violates a constraint becomes a catalog/eligibility defect, a new acceptance criterion and a regression task before expanding categories.

Run a counterbalanced shopping study against filters. Evaluate suitability, selection time, effort, and confidence before testing real checkout conversion.
